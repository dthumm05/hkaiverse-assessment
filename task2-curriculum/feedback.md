# Faculty and Graduate Feedback

## 1. Purpose

This document records the status of stakeholder feedback for the proposed B.Tech CSE curriculum review. The purpose is to distinguish feedback actually received from assumptions and recommendations that still require validation.

## 2. Feedback collection status

At the time of preparing this report, I have not conducted a formal interview with faculty members or graduates about the curriculum recommendations. I have also not received formal approval from a faculty member or process owner to conduct such fieldwork.

Therefore, this document does not present any invented interview responses, quotations, survey results, or stakeholder endorsements.

My preliminary analysis draws on my personal student experience, the available academic transcript information, selected public job descriptions, and AI-assisted suggestions that require independent verification. These sources provide preliminary context but do not substitute for formal stakeholder feedback or verification against official curriculum documents.

## 3. Preliminary student perspective

From my personal experience, assignment information may be shared through multiple channels, including email, SIS announcements, and WhatsApp groups. Submission methods can vary by course, and the availability of marks and feedback may not always be predictable.

These observations helped motivate the proposed Assignment Submission and Review Management System described in Task 1.

They are personal observations, not findings from a formal survey. I have not established how frequently these issues occur across the student population or whether faculty members experience the process in the same way.

## 4. Questions for future faculty and graduate feedback

If access becomes available, I would seek voluntary feedback on the following questions:

1. Which parts of the current curriculum best prepare students for practical software development?
2. Which technical skills do graduates feel they use most often in internships or jobs?
3. Are there topics that need more hands-on exercises, projects, or assessment?
4. How well do current projects develop debugging, testing, documentation, and teamwork skills?
5. Are the proposed curriculum updates feasible within the existing credit structure and available laboratory resources?
6. Would an integrated project based on assignment submission, review, and feedback be educationally useful?
7. What should be retained, updated, reduced, or added to the curriculum, and why?

Participation would be voluntary. Responses should be summarised without identifying participants unless they have explicitly agreed to be identified.

## 5. How feedback will inform the recommendations

Any future stakeholder feedback should be recorded with the date, participant category, consent status where applicable, and a concise summary of the response. Personal details should be excluded unless genuinely necessary and appropriately authorised.

Feedback should be compared with official course syllabi, assessment requirements, and public job-description evidence. A single person's opinion should not automatically be treated as representative of all students, faculty, or employers.

If stakeholder access remains unavailable, the curriculum recommendations will remain preliminary and will explicitly identify the need for further validation.

## 6. Current limitations

- No formal faculty or graduate interviews have been completed.
- No formal student survey has been completed for this curriculum review.
- Informal student observations have not been independently validated.
- No stakeholder endorsement of the proposed curriculum redesign is claimed.

## 7. Conclusion

Stakeholder feedback would help determine whether the proposed curriculum changes address real learning needs and can be implemented within institutional constraints. Until that feedback is obtained, the recommendations should be treated as a preliminary evidence-informed proposal rather than an approved curriculum change.
