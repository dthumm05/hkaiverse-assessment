## 4. Observed facts, assumptions, and unknowns

### Observed from personal experience

- Assignment information can be distributed across multiple communication channels.
- Submission methods vary across courses or assignments.
- Marks and feedback visibility can vary, and students may need to check or follow up.

These points describe my experience; they are not claims that every course follows the same process.

### Assumptions used to develop the proposal

- Students and faculty may benefit from a consolidated course-wise status view.
- Faculty may want a list of submissions awaiting review or release.
- A clear receipt and an explicit failure message may reduce uncertainty when an online submission fails.
- Configurable rules may be necessary because deadlines, late submissions, and resubmissions can differ by assignment.
- A named owner and explicit release permission may reduce confusion about responsibility.

These are design hypotheses, not confirmed findings. They need validation before implementation.

### Unknowns that must not be presented as facts

- The official process for creating and announcing assignments.
- Whether SIS, email, WhatsApp, Google Forms/Drive, or external learning platforms are officially approved for particular tasks.
- Who confirms receipt of handwritten or external-platform submissions.
- Who reviews work, enters marks, checks them, and authorises release.
- Whether there are standard deadlines for feedback or marks release.
- The actual frequency and causes of late, duplicate, missing, or failed submissions.
- The time currently spent by students and faculty on follow-ups.
- Whether an existing institutional system already provides some or all of the proposed functionality.

## 5. Proposed validation questions

If a faculty member or process owner agrees to speak with me, I would ask:

1. How are assignments currently created and announced, and which channel is considered authoritative?
2. Which submission methods are permitted for different assignment types?
3. How is receipt confirmed for handwritten, online, and external-platform submissions?
4. Who reviews submissions and enters marks or feedback?
5. Who checks and authorises the release of marks or feedback?
6. What rules apply to late submissions, corrections, and resubmissions?
7. What should happen when a submission is missing from the record, a portal fails, or a student disputes a status or mark?
8. Is there an existing system or report that already addresses these needs?
9. What is a reasonable way to measure student and staff time spent checking status or following up, without collecting private student data?

No answers to these questions are claimed in this report unless they are collected and dated later.

## 7. Current conclusion

The available evidence supports investigating whether fragmented assignment information, varied submission routes, and inconsistent marks visibility create avoidable uncertainty for students. It does **not** yet establish the size of the problem, its prevalence across the institute, the faculty-side process, or the amount of time that a proposed system would save.

The next useful step is to validate the current process with a willing process owner and, if permitted, record a small number of dated observations. Until then, the proposed faculty workflow and any benefits estimates must remain explicitly provisional.
