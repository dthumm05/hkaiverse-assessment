# Task 1 — Time-Saving Calculations

## 1. Purpose

This file estimates whether a unified assignment and assessment status view could reduce time spent checking submission status and following up about marks or feedback.

**Important:** These are illustrative scenarios, not measured results. I have not completed a formal time study or verified the assumptions with faculty. The values below demonstrate the calculation method and must be replaced with pilot measurements if access becomes available.

## 2. What Is Included

The estimate focuses on time spent on repeated status checks and follow-ups, minus estimated ongoing support effort.

It does **not** fully quantify:

- Initial design and development time
- Training and onboarding
- Long-term maintenance and hosting
- Faculty time spent entering or correcting records
- Faculty time saved, unless separately measured
- Costs associated with errors or delays

These calculations are a preliminary decision aid, not proof that the proposed system would save time overall.

## 3. Calculation Method

- **Current follow-up time per month** = Number of follow-ups × Average minutes per follow-up
- **Gross time saved** = Current follow-up time × Assumed percentage reduction
- **Net monthly time saved** = Gross time saved − Additional monthly support effort

A positive net value indicates estimated time saved after support effort. A negative value means the assumed support effort exceeds the estimated time saved.

## 4. Illustrative Scenarios

All inputs below are assumptions, not observed measurements.

| Scenario | Follow-ups/month | Minutes/follow-up | Current time/month | Assumed reduction | Gross time saved | Extra support time | Net time saved |
| -------- | ---------------: | ----------------: | -----------------: | ----------------: | ---------------: | -----------------: | -------------: |
| Low      |                4 |                 5 |             20 min |               50% |           10 min |             30 min |        -20 min |
| Middle   |               10 |                10 |            100 min |               50% |           50 min |             60 min |        -10 min |
| High     |               20 |                15 |            300 min |               50% |          150 min |             90 min |        +60 min |

### Worked Example: Middle Scenario

1. Assumed follow-ups per month = 10.
2. Assumed time per follow-up = 10 minutes.
3. Current follow-up time = 10 × 10 = **100 minutes per month**.
4. Assumed reduction = 50%.
5. Gross time saved = 100 × 0.50 = **50 minutes per month**.
6. Assumed additional support time = 60 minutes per month.
7. Net time saved = 50 − 60 = **−10 minutes per month**.

Under these assumptions, the middle scenario does not save time overall. A new system should not be recommended simply because it centralises information.

## 5. Interpretation

- The low scenario estimates a net loss of 20 minutes per month.
- The middle scenario estimates a net loss of 10 minutes per month.
- The high scenario estimates a net saving of 60 minutes per month.
- Results depend on follow-up frequency, time per follow-up, the reduction achieved, and ongoing support effort.
- This model does not establish which scenario reflects actual IFHE practice.
- It estimates time only; it does not assign a monetary value or prove improvements in student outcomes.

## 6. How to Measure This During a Pilot

If permission is available, collect a small, privacy-safe sample over a defined period.

Record:

- Number of assignment-status or marks-related follow-ups
- Approximate time spent on each follow-up
- Time spent maintaining the proposed status view
- Time spent resolving errors, duplicates, and access requests
- Whether a question was resolved without another follow-up
- Cases where the system displayed an incorrect or incomplete status

Compare a similar period before and during the pilot. Record the sample size, dates, measurement method, and differences that could affect the comparison.

Do not collect student names, IDs, marks, or private messages for this estimate.

## 7. Decision Rule

Continue with the proposed system only if a permitted pilot shows that it improves status clarity or reduces effort enough to justify the additional work, without creating unacceptable access, privacy, or accuracy risks.

If the existing tools already work well, or data-entry and support effort exceed the time saved, prefer a smaller intervention—such as one clearly maintained assignment-information page with links and status guidance—instead of building a separate application.

## 8. Limitations

- Scenario inputs are illustrative assumptions.
- The 50% reduction is assumed, not measured.
- Support-time estimates are assumed, not measured.
- The faculty-side workflow and effort have not been confirmed.
- Initial development, training, maintenance, and infrastructure costs are not fully included.
- Results may differ by course, submission method, class size, and faculty process.
- No pilot has been conducted, so no real-world time saving is claimed.

## 9. Conclusion

The calculation suggests that a unified status view could save time in a high-follow-up situation, but it could also add work in low- or medium-follow-up situations. A small, permitted pilot with measured inputs is needed before deciding whether to build or adopt the system.
